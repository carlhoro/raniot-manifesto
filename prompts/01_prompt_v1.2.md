Create a single-page, editorial-style personal website that functions as a professional manifesto.

Context:
This is not a services website, not a landing page, and not a commercial product.
The site represents a personal professional position, with RANIOT as the intellectual and technical initiative that supports the work, trajectory, and knowledge generated.

Tone and style:
- Minimalist
- Editorial
- Technical but accessible
- Serious, sober, and reflective
- No marketing language
- No startup or SaaS aesthetics
- Generous white space
- Clear typographic hierarchy
- Calm visual rhythm
- Designed for reading and thoughtful navigation

Typography:
- Use a neutral, Helvetica-like sans-serif font for all body text and headings
- Prioritize readability and long-form reading
- No decorative or expressive fonts
- The logo typography is not to be replicated in text; it should be treated as a visual mark only

Audience:
- Technical teams
- Digital transformation leaders
- People responsible for digital systems in companies
- Readers coming from LinkedIn and academic or professional publications

Color palette (strictly constrained):
- Primary color: dark graphite gray for main text and headings
- Secondary accent color: a muted technical blue inspired by the RANIOT logo (Pantone 7459 C)
- Secondary text color: medium gray for long-form reading
- Background: white or very light off-white

Color usage rules:
- The blue accent color must be used sparingly (links, subtle emphasis, separators)
- No gradients
- No bright or saturated colors
- No warm colors (orange, red, yellow)
- No dark backgrounds
- Overall appearance should feel almost monochromatic, with blue as a signal of rigor, not decoration

Page structure (single scroll page):

1. Hero / Declaration
   - Personal name as the main focus
   - RANIOT presented as a private initiative that bridges academic knowledge and real-world practice
   - One strong positioning sentence (not a slogan, not a value proposition)

2. Professional Position
   - A short section explaining a clear view on technology and digital transformation
   - Emphasis on criteria, planning, and intentionality
   - Critique of improvised or tool-driven digitalization

3. Digital Governance
   - Explain why digital governance must be planned
   - Governance before tools
   - Tools (such as Microsoft 365) mentioned only as enablers, never as solutions
   - Focus on structure, decision-making, and sustainability

4. IoT as a Bridge
   - IoT presented as the bridge between the physical world and digital systems
   - Emphasis on how businesses interact with reality through data
   - IoT framed as contextual infrastructure, not as a trend or gadget

5. Knowledge Dissemination
   - Explain the importance of disseminating technical and academic knowledge
   - Focus on translating papers and research into practical understanding
   - Simple language, practice-oriented perspective
   - Depth can exist outside the site (papers, repositories, analysis tools, LLMs)

6. References
   - A section with external links:
     - IoT public repository
     - Digital governance structure repository (informational, versioned, not code-heavy)
   - Clarify that these are reference materials, not services

7. Contact
   - A single, clear call to action
   - Direct contact email: carlos.hoyos@raniot.tech
   - No forms, no scheduling tools, no sales language

Constraints:
- Do NOT include services, pricing, testimonials, metrics, or marketing claims
- Do NOT include multiple CTAs
- Do NOT frame the content as a company offering
- The result should feel like a professional manifesto, not a corporate homepage

Technical assumptions:
- Use Next.js (App Router)
- Use Tailwind CSS
- Use a clean, readable layout suitable for long-form reading
